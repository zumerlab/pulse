Pulse

Why

States compete for attention. A home has lights, heating, locks, a media player, batteries running down; a car has a battery and tires; a server has load and disks. Most interfaces answer with a grid of cards and leave the sorting to you. Pulse sorts first, and uses shape to say what it sorted.

The idea in one sentence

Each system is a radial object with a few channels. The active channel sits in front; the others wait behind, larger and fainter. An alert brings its channel to the front and, once resolved, sends it back. A new system is only data.

Why round

A circle has a center and an edge, and that is all Pulse needs. The center holds the present: the name, the value, one line that says why. The edge holds the control: a band you drag, segments you tap, names along the ring you choose from. There is no corner to put a menu in, so nothing permanent competes with what is in front.

Round things also stack. Put a larger ring behind a smaller one and the eye reads depth without a shadow or a blur. That is the whole trick: the layers of a system are rings of growing size, each one a bit further back.

Depth has to mean something

Pulse uses depth for three things, in this order of importance.

  1. What is active. The front layer is the one you read and use.
  2. What is urgent. An alert raises its layer. A more serious one displaces it; a resolved one goes back on its own.
  3. How much room. A system comes closer when something needs focus, and steps back when nothing does.

Anything else that moves in z is decoration, and decoration is what tires people. There is no parallax for its own sake, no breathing for its own sake. The pointer tilts the object a few degrees so the layers read as layers, and that is all.

Several alerts

One alert is easy. Three at once is where interfaces usually start to shout. Pulse has five cumulative rules for that.

RuleWhat it does
StableThe one in front stays. Only a more serious one displaces it. The others wait in order of arrival.
One waveOnly the front alert calls. The ones waiting are their layers behind, in their color, named.
SeenAn alert you have seen goes behind and stops calling. It comes back if its text changes.
SummaryWith many alerts, the board's center counts them and cycles through them; you open one, resolve it, come back.
LockedAfter a layer change the controls lock for a moment, so a nervous tap does not resolve two things.

Against layer confusion

Layers in depth are easy to overdo. These are the rules that keep them readable, and the code enforces them.

  1. At most three visible layers. The rest are counted and named, not drawn. The viewer can raise it to five on a large screen.
  2. Only the front layer has a face and a full disc. Behind: a ring and a label.
  3. The front stroke is twice the one behind. The hierarchy reads without color.
  4. Labels are staggered, never on the same line.
  5. Color belongs to the alert: ring, segment, label. The disc is never painted.
  6. In segments, only the failing one is painted, and the call comes out of there.
  7. Never two layers at the same radius. Each step grows by at least 42 percent.
  8. The 3D container takes no clicks. The layers do. Tapping a ring brings it forward.

A control resolved on the face has no detail

A disc opens a detail only when the detail adds something the face does not have: a day of readings, a picture, a long list, a color wheel, a month, several entities to name. A dial you can drag on the ring, a mode you can pick on the ring, an alarm you can arm on the ring: those are done where they are. Opening something that repeats the face is a tap wasted and a step to come back.

What is shown is what was confirmed

A control beats while a command is on its way and settles when the source reports the new state. If the command is refused, the value comes back, the control keeps a red outline and a Retry appears. Pulse never pretends.

Two registers

A ring can be a gauge or an instrument. A gauge is read: thin strokes, tabular numbers, high contrast. An instrument is felt: filled arcs, overlap, asymmetry. Pulse is a gauge in front and an instrument behind: the layers behind are the ambient, the front layer is the data.

Nothing moves without a cause: a reading changed, something is urgent, you went somewhere. With prefers-reduced-motion the calls and the travel are gone; the hierarchy stays, by contrast and position.

How we got here

  1. Composite icons: a glyph and two related rings, not two random data points.
  2. Depth has to mean something: active, urgent, room.
  3. Layers seen head-on; the tilt only explains.
  4. Four domains, a home, a car, a pet, a server, and one engine for all of them.
  5. Waves instead of shakes: what needs attention calls.
  6. Several alerts, five rules.
  7. Home Assistant as the first real source, and everything it taught: rooms, scenes, the gear, the card.
