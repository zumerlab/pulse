In use

Your own source

You can connect Pulse to other sources through a host adapter. The homelab app reads a Mac or Linux machine through a small collector. The world app reads the calamities of the world (earthquakes, cyclones, floods, wildfires, volcanoes) from public feeds, with no server at all. The Signal K app reads a boat. The fleet app follows the trams of Helsinki from a public MQTT feed, the fleet as one object per dimension and one per vehicle. The Prometheus app reads a fleet of hosts with PromQL by configuration. The drones app flies a swarm of ArduPilot vehicles through mavlink2rest. The flights app reads the sky from the community ADS-B networks and OpenSky, the emergencies on a globe. The demo app uses simulated readings for a home, a car, a pet and a server. Each uses the same rings, layers and controls.

What a system is

A system is a title and a few channels. A channel reads the host's state and returns a reading, or nothing when there is nothing for it. A reading is one of three rings, a big value, one line, the things it depends on, and sometimes an alert with its actions. That is the whole contract between a host and the object.

{
  kind: 'p',                  // a band ('p'), switches ('s') or a choice ('c')
  v: 0.74,                    // the band's progress
  dial: { min: 0, max: 100, step: 1, unit: '%', label: 'charge', value: 74, id: 'battery', set: (v) => action },
  big: '74%', label: '310 km',
  ids: ['battery'],
  alert: { level: 1, big: '12%', label: 'battery', why: 'Not enough charge for tomorrow', verb: 'Schedule charging', acts: [action] },
}

Two to four channels that compete for attention, ordered front to back, make a good system. More than that is usually two systems.

What a host is

A host hands the board its systems, tells it when a thing changes, runs the actions the board hands back, and names the things. In the core it is one interface, PulseHost. The Home Assistant app makes one out of a source with three implementations (the WebSocket, the card's hass object, the demo homes); the homelab app makes one out of its collector; the world app out of public feeds it polls on their own clocks; the Signal K app out of a vessel's paths over a WebSocket stream; the fleet app out of vehicles over MQTT, with the vehicles as the board's second axis; the Prometheus app out of a configuration, every channel a query with its thresholds; the drones app out of a MAVLink stream, with commands back; the flights app out of the ADS-B networks, each question on its own clock.

Where it runs

The core is a plain module that mounts on an element and draws with CSS and a little SVG. It works inside a shadow root, which is what a Home Assistant card is, and on a round screen, which is what a boat's helm or a wall panel may be. The repository has nine examples: the Home Assistant app, the homelab app over a Mac or a Linux box, the world app over public feeds, the Signal K app over a boat, the fleet app over Helsinki's trams, the Prometheus app over a fleet of hosts, the drones app over a swarm, the flights app over the sky, and the demo app over made-up data. The steps, from the imports to the theme: In your project.
